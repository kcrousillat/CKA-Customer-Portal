# Pool finish chips

Cut from Florida Stucco's own brochures with `tools/chip-crop.py`, and committed
so Airtable can ingest them into the Palettes `Photo` field. Airtable copies an
image on ingest, so nothing here stays hot-linked.

**Florida Stucco**, 28 colors:

- **Florida Gem** (11) - color quartz in enriched cement
- **Pearl** (6) - natural pebble
- **Petite Pearl** (11) - the same pebble, smaller stone

**Diamond Brite** (made by SGM), 17 colors:

- **Diamond Brite** (11) - quartz aggregate in polymer-modified cement
- **Watercolors** (6) - their darker and tinted range

The Diamond Brite crops carry **two images each**, because the brochure shows
both: a photograph of the water over that finish, and the dry chip beside it.
Keeping the pair is more honest than either alone - the chip is the material,
the water is the thing an owner is actually choosing, and a quartz finish reads
nothing like its chip once there are four feet of water over it.

The brochures are flat scans with no text layer, so the chips cannot be found by
position the way a designer's spec book can. They are found by texture instead -
speckled aggregate against a flat page - and where a chip is too pale to
register (White Gem, Petite Silver) the grid is given explicitly.

**These are photographs of printed paper, twice removed from the product.** The
brochure says so itself: "actual finishes may vary slightly from the printing
process of the actual sample." They are for narrowing a choice, never for
settling one. A pool finish also shifts hard between dry, wet and under four
feet of water, and none of that is in a chip. The final pick comes off a
physical sample, outdoors.
